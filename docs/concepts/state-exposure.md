# State exposure

**State exposure** is the effective degree to which an event outcome affects the target state.

It asks not “How popular is this event?” but “How much of its price change truly contains information about the target state?”

## Components of exposure

State exposure has three main dimensions:

- **Causal strength:** whether the outcome's effect is direct, clear, and material.
- **Information independence:** whether it adds new state information rather than repeating another component.
- **Price quality:** whether the market price is observable, continuous, and sufficiently reliable.

## Exposure is not probability

A high event probability does not imply high state exposure.

An almost certain event with little impact may have low exposure. A lower-probability event that would materially change the state may have high exposure.

## Exposure and weight

The index converts component exposures into normalized weights. A weight determines how much an event's price change contributes to the period's index change.

State exposure is the economic meaning; weight is its calculation expression. They are related but not interchangeable.

## Changes over time

Effective exposure may change as time to expiry shortens, price quality declines, information is absorbed by other events, or the event loses real-world relevance.

Such changes should enter weight updates at preset review points rather than through arbitrary intraday adjustments.
