# Technology evolution indexes

Technology evolution indexes measure a critical technology's persistent movement from research breakthroughs toward deployment, diffusion, governance, and social adoption.

They are not technology-company equity indexes and do not directly measure corporate valuations. Components must affect the state through capability, availability, adoption, or the institutional environment.

## Possible dimensions

- core capability breakthroughs;
- commercial deployment and scaling;
- falling costs and infrastructure maturity;
- adoption by users, companies, or the public sector;
- regulatory permission, restrictions, and governance;
- safety incidents and public trust.

## Candidate examples

Benchmark breakthroughs, major launches, approvals, infrastructure milestones, adoption goals, or systemic safety events may qualify.

## Time horizon

Short-term market reactions and long-term diffusion effects may differ. The index must define its dominant horizon in advance and avoid mixing short-term competition with long-term evolution in direction decisions.

> This page describes an index-family framework, not the release of a specific technology evolution index.
