# Geopolitical order indexes

Geopolitical order indexes measure how conflict, cooperation, and stability arrangements among states, alliances, and non-state actors jointly shape persistent international states.

## Possible target states

- a regional peace process;
- alliance cohesion or weakening;
- sanctions and economic isolation;
- stability of negotiations and security arrangements;
- conflict escalation or de-escalation.

Each index must choose one directional target. A regional peace process and an alliance's influence are different states and should not be mixed without an explicit methodology.

## Candidate examples

Ceasefires, talks, military action, treaty ratification, sanctions changes, and third-party security guarantees can all create state exposure.

One event may have different direction and exposure for different target states. A military deployment may strengthen alliance cohesion while weakening regional peace.

## Main risks

Geopolitical events are highly contextual and prone to jumps, incomplete information, and multiple causal mechanisms. They require stricter direction consistency, source verification, and exceptional-event rules.

> This page describes an index-family framework, not the release of a specific geopolitical order index.
