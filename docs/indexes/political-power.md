# Political power indexes

Political power indexes measure changes in a political actor's ability to gain, retain, transfer, or lose institutional power.

Political power is not the same as fame, polling attention, or one election probability. The target state must define what power is measured, within which institutional scope, and through what mechanism.

## Possible dimensions

- gaining or retaining executive authority;
- obtaining legislative control;
- forming a stable governing coalition;
- advancing key appointments or policy agendas;
- retaining leadership within a party or institution.

## Candidate examples

Election results, legislative seats, coalition formation, appointments, impeachment or removal proceedings, and judicial decisions that change institutional control may create valid exposure.

## Avoid duplicate weighting

Political markets often contain highly related propositions, such as candidate, party-control, and state-result markets for the same election.

The index must identify shared preconditions and avoid counting the same political outcome multiple times at full weight.

> This page describes an index-family framework, not the release of a specific political power index.
