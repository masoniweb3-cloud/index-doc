# Country development indexes

Country development indexes measure how related events continuously reprice the long-term development state of a country or economy.

They are not a single economic statistic or a subjective score of national value. They represent directional market judgments about defined development mechanisms.

## Possible dimensions

- economic growth and productive capacity;
- institutional execution and policy continuity;
- industrial upgrading and critical infrastructure;
- external financing, trade, and market access;
- human capital and technology absorption.

A specific index should define a focused target state rather than merging every dimension indiscriminately.

## Candidate examples

Major policy passage, international arrangements, critical-infrastructure delivery, trade restrictions, or structurally important industrial milestones may be relevant.

An event is eligible only when it has a clear outcome–mechanism–development-state path.

## Main boundaries

Short-term sentiment, isolated political news, or events without a direct development mechanism should not be included merely because they concern the same country.

Different countries cannot be assumed to use identical event types and weights because institutions, economic bases, and external constraints differ.

> This page describes an index-family framework, not the release of a specific country development index.
