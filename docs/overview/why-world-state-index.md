# Why do we need world-state indexes?

Prediction markets are good at answering specific questions: Will a bill pass? Will a negotiation reach an agreement? Will a product launch on time?

What people often care about, however, is not one event but the persistent change behind it.

| What a single event answers | What people really want to understand |
| --- | --- |
| Will a ceasefire agreement be signed? | Is the peace process improving or deteriorating? |
| Will a key bill pass? | Is a country's development momentum strengthening? |
| Will a product launch on time? | Is a technology accelerating in adoption? |
| Will a candidate win an election? | Is a political force expanding? |

A single market can price an event, but it cannot answer these broader questions on its own.

## A single event is only one piece

A peace process is not determined by one ceasefire, and technology adoption is not determined by one product launch. Important real-world trends are shaped by many events at once, each revealing only part of the picture.

Watching one market can cause a local change to be mistaken for the overall trend.

## A state does not expire with one contract

Event contracts have fixed deadlines. When the outcome is known, the market settles and ends.

Peace processes, national development, and technological evolution do not stop when one contract settles. New negotiations, policies, and milestones continue to appear.

This creates a maturity mismatch: expiring event markets are being used to observe real-world trends that do not expire.

## “Up” can mean opposite things across events

A rising probability of a ceasefire usually strengthens a peace process; a rising probability of military escalation weakens it. Both contract prices may rise, but their meanings for the same state are opposite.

Related markets therefore cannot simply be averaged. Direction must be aligned first, and then each event's importance to the target state must be evaluated.

## What Signa Index solves

Signa Index organizes these scattered, short-lived, and differently directed signals:

- identify events that genuinely affect the target state;
- map positive and negative events onto a common direction;
- give more weight to direct, high-quality information;
- replace expired events with new ones while the index continues.

The result is not an isolated event probability but an index series that continuously records changes in a real-world state.

## What does it make possible?

Researchers can observe how markets reassess long-term trends. Index products can reference one public rule set. Users can express either a strengthening or weakening view on the same state.

Prediction markets price individual events; Signa Index organizes those events into a real-world trend.
